@subtitle By a Passenger · 1911

# Wednesday

We went aboard at eleven and were out of the river by one. The band played on the after deck while the tugs pulled us about, and a great many people on the quay waved at persons they could not possibly see.

Our stateroom is smaller than the advertisement, but very clean. The steward calls me Madam as if he meant it.

# Thursday

A head sea. I did not go down to dinner, and do not propose to say why.

The doctor tells me the second day is always the worst. I understand he says this on every day of the voyage.

# Friday

Better. Took my chair on the Promenade Deck and was wrapped in a rug by the Deck Steward, like a parcel for the post. Beef tea at eleven.

The gentleman in the next chair has been reading the same page of the same novel since breakfast. At four he closed it with every sign of satisfaction.

# Saturday

Fog from the forenoon. The whistle went every two minutes all the afternoon and all the night.

One counts it as one counts a clock striking in another room, and at last one notices it only when it is late. From the rail there is nothing to be seen but the water close alongside, very black.

# Sunday

Service in the Saloon. The Commander read the prayers in the voice he must use for giving orders, and it suited them very well.

In the afternoon the fog lifted all at once, and there was a sailing-ship quite near, every sail set, going the other way. Everybody ran to the side. She did not appear to notice us.

# Monday

The concert. A young lady sang two songs and was made to sing a third. A gentleman recited something long about a lighthouse.

The pool on the day's run was won by a clergyman, who gave it to the Orphans and looked as though he had not quite meant to.

# Tuesday

Gulls this morning, three of them, close astern and quarrelling over what the cooks throw out. The quartermaster says that means land within a day.

At four o'clock, a grey line to the north-west that was not cloud.

I have kept this journal for nobody in particular. If it has come to rest in a ship's library, let it stay there for the next who crosses.
