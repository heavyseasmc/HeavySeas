@subtitle The Mate's Boat
@motto The first of the tales, reprinted for the ship's library.

# I

The barque went down on the Banks in a fog so thick that the men in the boat never saw her go. They heard her, which was worse.

Then there was only the swell, and the drip from the oars, and the men looking at the mate to see what he would do.

# II

He did a small thing first. He took the water breaker between his knees and sat on it.

"A dipper a man at sunrise," he said, "and one at sunset. Any man who wants more may ask me, and I shall say no."

Nobody laughed, but one or two sat back on the thwarts, as if that settled something.

# III

For two days they pulled by the compass and saw nothing but grey. The cook prayed aloud, and the boy asked what the compass was for, if there was nowhere to go.

On the second night a man reached for the breaker while the mate slept. The mate was not asleep. He said nothing about it in the morning, and nobody tried again.

# IV

On the third morning a gull came out of the fog and went once round the boat.

"Land birds don't come out this far," said the cook.

"That's a sea bird," said the mate, "but it sleeps ashore."

He put the boat's head the way it had gone, and they pulled as they had not pulled since the barque went down.

# V

By noon there were gulls on every side, and the swell had the short, ugly look the sea has over shoal ground. At three they heard surf. At four they were in a cove, with a cottage above it and smoke going up from the chimney.

The mate stood up and looked into the breaker. It was still a quarter full.

"Well," he said, "I was too careful."

It is the only joke anybody remembers him making.
