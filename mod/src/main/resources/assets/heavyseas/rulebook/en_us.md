# Chapter 1: The Night {#section-0}

The mirror has taken you back to the night of 14 April 1912. In a few hours the Northern Star will go down, and you will be squeezed into a small boat with a handful of strangers, as one of the people aboard it.

## What You Do in a Game {#section-1}

- There are 6 to 8 people in the boat, and each of you plays one character. You only find out who you are when you wake up in the fog.
- Each day, gulls may fly in out of the fog. As soon as there are 4 gulls, the boat has reached land and the game is over.
- If everyone in the boat dies, the game is over too.
- At the end, everyone adds up their own score, and the highest score wins. If several people share the highest score, they all win.

## Surviving Is Not Winning {#section-2}

Your score comes from four things, and each one counts on its own:

1. you are still alive at the end;
2. the treasure you are carrying;
3. the person you secretly love is still alive at the end;
4. the person you secretly hate is dead.

At the start, everyone is given one Love card and one Hate card. Each names a character, and only you can see yours. They stay hidden until the very end, when they are turned over in the Reveal.

So while you help keep the boat going until it reaches land, you are also quietly protecting the person you love and working against the person you hate. It is best not to let anyone see whom you are protecting, or whom you are after.

## How Long a Game Lasts {#section-3}

There is no limit on the number of days. When the gulls reach 4 depends on which navigation cards come up and on what the helmsman picks, so most games last about ten days.

# Chapter 2: Before You Board {#section-4}

## Through the Mirror {#section-5}

Find the Magic Mirror in your own world and right-click it to come over. The Northern Star looks after your inventory while you are away, and you arrive in front of the mirror on the A Deck landing.

When you want to go home, right-click the mirror aboard the Northern Star. You get your inventory back and arrive in front of the mirror you came through. If that mirror is no longer there, you are sent to your respawn point instead.

The Northern Star is everyone's lobby. You cannot break any blocks aboard, but you are free to walk around and look at the ship.

## Signing Up {#section-6}

Boat No. 1 hangs on the boat deck. It is the drill boat, and it has 8 seats. Sitting down in it signs you up for the next game; standing up again cancels.

## Ringing the Bell {#section-7}

Once 6 to 8 people are seated in the drill boat, anyone sitting in it can ring the bell beside it (or right-click the boat again). If there are too few people or too many, you are only told how many are seated, and the boat does not leave.

Whoever rings the bell chooses the roster for this game. You can use one of the ready-made 6, 7 or 8-player rosters, or pick the characters yourself, as long as there are as many characters as people sitting in the boat and no character appears twice.

@presets

Once the roster is settled, the characters are dealt out at random to the people in the boat. Only the people sitting in the drill boat when it leaves are taken into the game.

## When a Game Is Over {#section-8}

After the Reveal, everyone is brought back to the Northern Star. If you want to play again, just sit down in the drill boat again.

# Chapter 3: The Eight in the Boat {#section-9}

## Seats {#section-10}

Counting from bow to stern, every character has a fixed place in the seating order, and that order stays the same whoever plays the character. In a smaller game some seat numbers are left empty, but that does not matter: only who sits ahead of whom counts.

## Size and Survival {#section-11}

Every character has two numbers: Size and Survival.

**Size** governs four things:

- How strong you are in a fight. Even when you are hurt and have lost health, your fighting strength does not drop.
- Your maximum health. You start with health equal to your Size, and every point of damage takes away 1 health.
- How many points the person who hates you scores once you are dead.
- How often navigation cards name you for thirst: the bigger you are, the more often it happens.

**Survival** governs two things:

- How many points you score yourself if you are alive at the end.
- How many points the person who loves you scores while you are alive.

An easy way to remember it: whoever wants you alive scores your Survival; whoever wants you dead scores your Size.

## The Eight {#section-12}

@roster jeweler

While the Jewellery set is on him, in his hand or in front of him, it scores double. A full set of 3 is normally worth 8 points; on the Jeweller it is worth 16.

@roster collector

Fine Art scores double while it is on him.

@roster captain

Bundles of Cash score double while they are on him.

@roster mate

He has no special ability, but he is the biggest person in the boat.

@roster hostess

- Whenever thirst is resolved, she always goes last, whether she is conscious or not.
- Cadging water: while thirst is being resolved for a navigation card, every Water drunk by someone who goes before her cancels 1 of her thirst. Water that one person passes to another counts too, and it all adds up.
- Cadging rum: whenever someone else drinks Rum, she gets a sip as well, which gives her Size +3 in fights that day and also 1 thirst that day. However many people drink that day, it only counts once; and if she drinks her own Rum as well, it is still only +3.
- Both of these only work while she is conscious.

@roster sailor

When he goes overboard, he does not take the 1 damage for falling in. He has to be conscious for this; an unconscious Sailor is hurt as usual. The sharks drawn by Chum get him like anyone else.

@roster doctor

When he plays a Medical Kit himself, it is not discarded: it stays in front of him and he can use it again later, though each use still takes his action for that day. If he gives a Medical Kit to someone else, they discard it after use as normal.

@roster kid

He steals by different rules from everyone else:

- he can only take from the other person's hand, one card at random, and cannot choose a card that is face up in front of them;
- the other person cannot refuse, so his stealing never leads to a fight;
- when he goes to pick his target, there is no warning at all.

If the other person has no cards in hand, he gets nothing.

# Chapter 4: The Two in Your Heart {#section-13}

At the start, everyone is given two secret cards, one Love and one Hate. Each names a character, and only you can see yours.

- Every character is loved by exactly one person and hated by exactly one person. So if you work out who loves someone, you know nobody else in the boat does.
- You might draw yourself.
- The person you love and the person you hate might be the same person.

These two cards never change during the game, and nobody else sees them until they are turned over one by one in the Reveal. For how they score, see Chapter 11.

# Chapter 5: How a Day Goes {#section-14}

## The Four Phases {#section-15}

Every day in the boat runs through these steps in order:

1. **Weather**: turn over a weather card. It lasts for the whole day.
2. **Provision**: the Supply Crate is passed from bow to stern, and each person keeps 1 provision.
3. **Action**: starting from the bow, each conscious person takes a turn to do one thing.
4. **Navigation**: the helmsman picks a navigation card to play, and it is resolved in the order gulls → overboard → thirst.

Once navigation is resolved, the day is over, and the next day starts again by turning over a weather card. The very first day also starts with the weather.

## In Your Hand and in Front of You {#section-16}

Your provisions are kept in two places:

- **In your hand**: these are your cards in hand, and only you can see them.
- **In front of you**: these are out in the open, where everyone in the boat can see them. Moving a card from your hand to in front of you is called **laying it in front**.

Laying a card in front does not use your action and can be done in any phase, but once a card is in front of you it cannot go back into your hand. The one exception: when someone steals from you and reaches the moment of choosing a card, you can no longer lay cards in front.

Laying cards in front has an upside, because many provisions only work when they are in front of you. It also has a cost:

- if you go overboard, the waves wash away every card in front of you, except a Life Preserver laid in front;
- when someone steals from you, they can choose any particular card from in front of you.

## What Others Can See {#section-17}

Others can see your health, every card laid in front of you, and how many cards you have in hand (but not what they are). Nobody but you can see your Love and Hate, or what the cards in your hand are.

## Giving Cards {#section-18}

During the action phase, as long as you are conscious, you can give cards to others at any time, as many as you like, and it does not use your action.

- A card you give from your hand goes straight into the other person's hand; a card you give from in front of you goes in front of them and stays face up.
- You can give to anyone still in the boat, even someone unconscious or dead.
- If two people want to trade, each of them simply gives the other a card.
- While a fight is under way, no cards can change hands at all.

You cannot give cards in the provision or navigation phase. The navigation phase has just two exceptions: throwing a Life Preserver to someone overboard, and passing Water to someone who is thirsty (see Chapter 9).

## Who Is 'Nearest the Bow' {#section-19}

Passing the crate, working out whose turn it is to act, and deciding who is helmsman all count only people who are conscious and online right now. Anyone unconscious, dead or disconnected is skipped, but they still keep their seats.

The seating order goes by where people are actually sitting now. As soon as someone swaps seats, the order behind them changes with it.

Thirst is the one exception: it is resolved in the characters' fixed order, and the Hostess always goes last.

# Chapter 6: Provisions {#provisions}

## The Supply Crate {#section-21}

- At the start of the provision phase, the conscious person nearest the bow opens the Supply Crate: they draw as many cards from the provision deck as there are conscious people in the boat right now.
- They keep 1 card in their hand and pass the rest on to the next person. Everyone keeps 1 like this, all the way to the end.
- Only the person holding the crate can see what is in it, but everyone can see how far it has been passed and how many cards are left in it.
- The passing order is settled the moment the crate is opened. Even if someone falls unconscious along the way, the crate does not change course.

## Time Limit {#section-22}

Whoever holds the crate is on a timer: 2 seconds for each card left in it, but never less than 20 seconds. If time runs out before you choose, the card you are looking at is kept for you.

## When the Deck Runs Out, It Is Gone {#section-23}

The provision deck is never shuffled back. If there are not enough cards left to go round, draw what is left, and the people at the end of the line get none. Once the deck is completely empty, the provision phase is skipped every day from then on.

If the day's weather is Sunday, then after the crate has gone round once, there is a whole second round.

# Chapter 7: Actions {#actions}

## Whose Turn {#section-25}

The next turn always goes to whoever is sitting nearest the bow, conscious, online and has not acted yet today. Each person gets only one action a day.

The order is live: if someone who has not acted yet is swapped up to the front, ahead of everyone else still waiting, their turn comes next; someone who has already acted and is then moved further back does not get a second turn today; and someone revived during the day who has not acted yet can still take a turn that day.

You have 60 seconds when your turn comes. If time runs out before you choose, you do nothing today.

## Five Choices {#section-26}

When it is your turn, choose one of these five things:

1. **Row**
2. **Swap seats**
3. **Steal**
4. **Use a provision**
5. **Do nothing**

## Row {#row}

- When you row, you first draw 2 cards from the navigation deck. If you have Oars face up in front of you, draw 1 more for each Oar.
- After looking at them, you put 1 of them face down on the rowing pile, and the rest go back to the bottom of the navigation deck.
- Only you know which card you put down, but everyone knows how many cards are in the rowing pile.
- Anyone who rows is marked as having rowed for the rest of the day, which may count when thirst is resolved (see Chapter 9).

You have 20 seconds to choose. If time runs out before you choose, all the cards you drew go back to the bottom of the deck and none go on the rowing pile, but you still count as having rowed today.

On a Becalmed day, nobody can row.

## Swap Seats {#section-28}

1. After choosing Swap seats, go back to the world, look at the person you want to swap with, and right-click them.
2. If they are conscious, they are asked whether they agree. If they agree, you swap seats at once; if they refuse, a fight breaks out on the spot, and you only swap if you win.
3. If they are unconscious, or a corpse, they count as agreeing, and you swap at once.

You cannot pick yourself, or anyone who has already been taken by the sea.

## Steal {#steal}

1. After choosing Steal, go back to the world, look at the person you want to steal from, and right-click them.
2. If they are conscious, they are asked whether they agree. If they refuse, a fight breaks out on the spot, and if you lose, you get nothing.
3. If they agree, if you win the fight, or if they are unconscious or a corpse, you get to choose one of their cards:
   - either name one of the cards face up in front of them, which then goes in front of you, still face up;
   - or draw one at random from their hand, which goes straight into your hand.

If they have no cards at all, you get nothing. You have 20 seconds to choose; if time runs out before you do, one card is drawn at random from their hand for you, and if their hand is empty, you take the first card in front of them.

The Child steals by different rules from everyone else; see Chapter 3.

## Picking a Target {#section-30}

After choosing Swap seats or Steal, you go back to the world and right-click the person you want. You have 20 seconds.

- During this time everyone in the boat can see you glowing and hears an announcement that you want to swap seats or steal, but nobody knows yet whom you are after. This is the time for everyone to bargain.
- When the Child goes to steal, he does not glow and nothing is announced.
- Before you pick someone, you can cancel and go back to choosing an action, and it does not use up your action for today.
- If you pick nobody within 20 seconds, you do nothing today.

Once you have right-clicked someone, it uses up your action for the day, whatever happens next.

## Use a Provision {#section-31}

Of all the provisions, only four are used by spending your action: the Medical Kit, the Parasol (to open it), the Shared Ration and the Flare Gun (as a signal). These four can be played straight from your hand or from in front of you. No other provision can be used through this choice.

For how each one works, see Chapter 12.

## Do Nothing {#section-32}

You sit this turn out, and that is it for today.

## Three Things That Do Not Use Your Action {#section-33}

These three things do not use your action:

- **Laying in front**: you can lay a card from your hand in front of you at any time (see Chapter 5).
- **Giving cards**: anyone conscious can do this at any time during the action phase (see Chapter 5).
- **Drinking Rum**: you can drink at any time, once per person per day (see Chapter 12).

# Chapter 8: Fights {#fights}

## When a Fight Breaks Out {#section-35}

A fight only ever breaks out in these three cases:

- the person you want to swap seats with is conscious and refuses;
- the person you want to steal from is conscious and refuses;
- someone plays the Shared Ration and another person objects.

You cannot start a fight just because you dislike someone.

## First, an Answer {#consent}

The person targeted has 20 seconds to decide whether to agree. If time runs out before they answer, they count as agreeing.

## Taking Sides {#taking-sides}

Until both sides are settled and the fight begins, anyone in the boat who is conscious, online and not already in the fight may join a side.

- People who have already acted today can join, and so can people who have nothing to do with the matter.
- Joining a side does not use your action.
- Once you have joined, you cannot leave or switch sides. Just saying you are neutral counts for nothing.
- The unconscious and the dead cannot join.

Taking sides starts with 20 seconds, and each time someone joins, the timer is reset to 8 seconds.

Everyone can see who is on each side and what each side's Size adds up to.

## Committing Weapons {#weapons}

Once the sides are settled, everyone in the fight can commit weapons.

- A weapon you commit stays face down for now: everyone only hears that 1 card was committed, not which weapon it is.
- Only weapons committed now count. Even if you already have a weapon face up in front of you, it adds nothing to your strength unless you commit it.
- The moment the fight is over, all committed weapons are shown: the ones already in front of you are used first, and any others are turned up from your hand and placed in front of you, where they stay face up from then on.

Committing weapons starts with 20 seconds, and each time someone commits one, the timer is reset to 6 seconds.

## Who Wins {#section-39}

Each side's strength = the Size of everyone on that side + the Rum they drank today + the weapons they committed.

- Everyone's Size counts in full; being hurt and losing health does not reduce it at all.
- If the two sides come out equal, the side that was targeted wins.
- On the losing side, everyone in the fight takes 1 damage. The winning side takes none.

After the fight:

- If the attacking side wins: a seat swap happens at once, a steal goes on to choosing a card, and a Shared Ration takes effect as normal.
- If the targeted side wins: the matter is dropped.

Win or lose, everyone who was in the fight (including anyone who took sides to help) is marked as having fought for the rest of the day.

If a Flare Gun was committed, it is discarded once the fight is over; other weapons stay in front of their owners.

## What Else You Can Do in a Fight {#section-40}

During a fight you can still lay cards in front (except the person being robbed, once the steal reaches the moment of choosing a card), and you can drink Rum, but nobody can give cards.

# Chapter 9: Navigation {#navigation}

As soon as the action phase ends, the navigation phase begins. Who goes into the water, who gets thirsty and whether any gulls come are all settled in this phase.

## The Helmsman {#section-42}

The helmsman is whoever is sitting nearest the stern and is conscious and online at the time. The helmsman is not fixed: if someone swaps seats, or the person at the stern falls unconscious, the helmsman changes with them.

## Picking a Navigation Card {#navigation-pick}

- Everyone who rowed today has put one navigation card face down on the rowing pile. Now the helmsman picks up the whole pile, looks through it and chooses one card to play.
- If the helmsman has a Compass in hand or in front of them, and the rowing pile already has cards in it, they draw one more card from the top of the navigation deck into the rowing pile before choosing. If the rowing pile is empty, the Compass is no use.
- It does not matter whether the helmsman rowed today; they still choose.
- If nobody rowed today and the rowing pile is empty, turn over the top card of the navigation deck and play that.
- If the rowing pile has cards but nobody in the boat is conscious any more, also turn over the top card of the deck and play that.

The helmsman has 20 seconds to choose. If time runs out before they decide, the card they are looking at is played.

Everyone sees the card that is played. The cards not chosen go back to the bottom of the navigation deck, and nobody but the helmsman knows what they were. Navigation cards always go back to the bottom of the deck after use, so the deck never runs out.

## What Is on a Navigation Card {#section-44}

Every navigation card has three sections, and they are always resolved in this order:

1. **Gulls**: whether there is one more gull, one fewer, or no change;
2. **Overboard**: who goes into the water;
3. **Thirst**: who gets thirsty.

Some cards also carry an oar icon or a fight icon. These icons only affect thirst; see below. For how to read a navigation card, see Chapter 14.

## Gulls {#section-45}

The gull count starts at 0 and can never go below 0. The moment it reaches 4, the boat comes ashore and the game ends on the spot, and the overboard and thirst sections of that card are not resolved.

## Overboard {#overboard}

- Whoever the navigation card names goes into the water. It may name certain characters, everyone in the boat, or whoever drank Rum today. If it names a character who is not in this game, that part is simply ignored; if it names a corpse in the boat, the corpse goes into the water too.
- Everyone who goes into the water takes 1 damage, except two kinds of people: a conscious Sailor, and anyone with a Life Preserver laid in front of them. A Life Preserver still clutched in your hand does not count.
- When someone goes into the water, every card in front of them is washed away into the discard pile, an open Parasol included, except a Life Preserver laid in front. The cards in their hand are not affected.

At the very moment people go into the water, the conscious people in the boat can still do two things. Whenever anyone holds a card they could use, this moment lasts 20 seconds:

- **Throw a Life Preserver**: you can throw a Life Preserver from your hand or from in front of you to a conscious person in the water (yourself included), as long as they do not already have one in front of them. An unconscious person cannot catch it; only a Life Preserver already laid in front of them when they fall protects them.
- **Play Chum**: playing Chum draws sharks, and everyone going overboard in this group takes 1 more damage. The Chum is discarded afterwards. Chum already face up in front of someone in the water takes effect without anyone playing it, even if that someone is you. Neither the Sailor nor a Life Preserver protects against the sharks; and even if several Chum take effect together, everyone still takes only 1 more damage.

The water is far more dangerous than the boat:

- If your health drops to exactly 0 after the damage and you have no Life Preserver in front of you, you drown outright instead of just falling unconscious.
- If your health drops below 0, you drown whether or not you have a Life Preserver.

Those who drown are taken by the sea, the cards in their hand and in front of them sink with them, and they are gone for the rest of the game. Those who do not drown climb back into the boat, take their old seats, and are still in time for this card's thirst.

## Thirst {#thirst}

Five things can make you thirsty today. Each of them counts at most once a day, but several of them can stack:

- you rowed today, and the navigation card played carries the oar icon;
- you fought today (taking sides to help someone else counts), and the card carries the fight icon;
- the thirst section of the card names you;
- you drank Rum today (this one ignores the card: if you drank, you are thirsty);
- today's weather is Scorching Heat.

For each thirst, drinking 1 Water deals with it; if you do not drink, you take 1 damage. Whether to drink and how many is entirely up to you, and you can drink Water from your hand or from in front of you.

- When your turn to resolve thirst comes, other conscious people can pass you Water to cover your thirst.
- Unconscious people still get thirsty, but they cannot drink on their own, so they rely on others to pass them Water. The dead do not get thirsty.
- An open Parasol in front of you covers one thirst for you each day.
- The Hostess always resolves last. Every Water drunk by someone before her cancels one of her thirst today.

Thirst is resolved one person at a time, in the characters' fixed order, not by where people are sitting now. Anyone who is not thirsty at all today is skipped and does not have to wait. Each person has 20 seconds; if time runs out before you confirm, you drink the number of Water you have selected.

## What the Weather Changes {#section-48}

The weather can change the rules of the navigation phase: in Dense Fog the gulls do not count, in Rain nobody is thirsty, on a Becalmed day there is no navigation phase at all, and in a Gale an extra navigation card is resolved… See Chapter 13 for the details.

## The Day Ends {#section-49}

Once the navigation phase is resolved, the day is over. Everyone's marks for having rowed, fought or drunk Rum today are cleared, but an open Parasol stays open. Then the next day starts with the weather.

# Chapter 10: Health, Unconsciousness and Death {#health}

## Three States {#section-51}

You start with health equal to your Size. After that, every point of damage takes away 1 health.

- Health above 0: you are **conscious**.
- Health exactly 0: you are **unconscious**.
- Health below 0: you are **dead**.

In the water there is one more rule: if your health drops to exactly 0 after the damage and you have no Life Preserver in front of you, you drown outright (see Chapter 9).

## Unconscious {#section-52}

An unconscious person:

- cannot act, cannot refuse anyone who wants to swap seats with them or steal from them, and cannot fight;
- cannot give cards, lay cards in front or drink Rum, and cannot drink Water on their own;
- still gets thirsty, and relies on others to pass them Water;
- counts as agreeing when someone swaps seats with them or steals from them;
- can be revived with a Medical Kit.

An unconscious Sailor is still hurt when he goes overboard. Anyone unconscious who goes overboard without a Life Preserver in front of them to protect them drowns.

## Healing {#healing}

- **Medical Kit**: one person regains 1 health. You can treat yourself, or revive someone unconscious, but only someone who is wounded and still alive can be treated.
- **Shared Ration**: every conscious, wounded person in the boat regains 1 health. The unconscious regain nothing and are not revived.

The dead cannot be brought back. Damage only ever goes down through healing; it never wears off on its own.

## Corpses in the Boat {#section-54}

Anyone who dies in the boat leaves a corpse behind:

- the corpse still occupies its seat;
- others can still swap seats with it or steal from it, and it cannot refuse;
- the treasure on the corpse still counts towards that player's score at the end;
- the Shared Ration can only be played while there is a corpse in the boat.

If a navigation card later sends a corpse overboard, it is taken by the sea along with all its cards.

## Disconnecting {#section-55}

If you disconnect, your wounds stay as they are and your ability keeps working. But while you are disconnected you cannot act: your turn in the action phase is skipped; if the Supply Crate is in your hands or you are the helmsman, the default is chosen for you when time runs out; and if you are thirsty, you can only wait for others to pass you Water.

## Only One Left {#section-56}

Even if only one person in the boat is still alive, the game does not end there.

# Chapter 11: Landfall and the Reveal {#scoring}

## When the Game Ends {#section-58}

The game only ends in one of these two ways:

- The gulls reach 4 and the boat comes ashore. This can happen in the navigation phase, or in the action phase when someone fires a Flare Gun.
- Everyone in the boat is dead.

As soon as the game ends, the Reveal begins.

## The Reveal {#section-59}

1. First, **Hate**: in order of final score from lowest to highest, each person's Hate card is turned over, one at a time, showing whom they hated.
2. Then **Love**: in the same order, each person's Love card is turned over, one at a time, showing whom they loved.

Both of everyone's cards are turned over, even for people taken by the sea. Where scores are equal, cards are turned over in final seat order.

After the cards comes the scoreboard: everyone can see each person's total and who scored highest, but only you can see the breakdown of your own four scores.

## Scoring {#section-60}

Score the four parts separately, then add them up for your total:

1. **You survived**: if you are still alive, score your own Survival. Unconscious counts as alive.
2. **Treasure**: as long as your body is still in the boat (alive, or as a corpse), all the treasure in your hand and in front of you scores. If you were taken by the sea, your treasure scores 0.
3. **Your beloved survived**: if the person you love is alive at the end, score their Survival, whether or not you are alive yourself.
4. **Your enemy died**: if the person you hate is dead, score their Size. It counts wherever they died, even if they were taken by the sea.

For what each treasure is worth, see Chapter 12.

## Special Cases {#section-61}

- **You love yourself**: parts 1 and 3 above both use your own Survival. If you are alive, you score it twice.
- **You love and hate the same person**: if they are alive you score part 3 (their Survival); if they are dead you score part 4 (their Size). You can only score one of the two.
- **You hate yourself**: you do not score part 1, even if you are alive. Instead, part 4 becomes the total Size of every other corpse in the boat, not counting yourself or the person you love, and not counting anyone taken by the sea.
- **You both love and hate yourself**: your Survival counts only once.

## Who Wins {#section-62}

The highest total wins. If several people share the highest total, they all win.

# Chapter 12: The Provision Cards {#cards}

@provision-total

Each provision below starts with the line printed on its card, followed by the details. 'In hand' and 'in front' tell you where the card has to be for it to be used.

## Consumables {#section-64}

@provision water

- You can drink it whether it is in your hand or in front of you.
- You can pass it to someone else while their thirst is being resolved.
- It is discarded once drunk.

@provision medical_kit

- Playing it uses your action. Choose a wounded, living person to regain 1 health: it can be you, and it can be someone unconscious, who is revived.
- It is discarded after use, except when the Doctor uses it himself: then it stays in front of him.

@provision bait_bucket

- Play it at the moment people go overboard: everyone going overboard in this group takes 1 more damage. Any conscious person can play it; you do not have to be in the water yourself. It is discarded once played.
- If it is face up in front of someone who goes overboard, it takes effect without anyone playing it, even if that someone is you. Afterwards it is washed away with the other cards in front of them.
- Neither the Sailor's ability nor a Life Preserver protects against it. Even if several take effect together, everyone still takes only 1 more damage. If more than one group goes overboard in a day, each group counts separately.

@provision ration

- Playing it uses your action, and it can only be played while there is a corpse in the boat: an unconscious person is not a corpse, and nor is anyone taken by the sea.
- It is discarded as soon as it is played. Then every other conscious person is asked, one by one, whether they object.
- If nobody objects: every conscious, wounded person in the boat regains 1 health.
- If someone objects: a fight breaks out on the spot, with you as the attacking side and the objector as the targeted side. The Ration only takes effect if you win; if you lose, it is wasted and you do not get the card back either.

## Equipment {#section-65}

@provision rum

- Drinking Rum does not use your action. You can drink it at any time, but only once per person per day.
- Once you drink, you get Size +3 in fights that day; and you always get 1 thirst that day, whatever icons the navigation card played shows.
- Rum is not discarded when you drink it: it stays in front of you (if you drink from your hand, it is laid in front first), and you can drink it again tomorrow.
- A conscious Hostess gets a sip too; see Chapter 3.

@provision compass

- A Compass is only useful to the helmsman, and it counts even in the helmsman's hand. As long as the rowing pile has cards in it that day, the helmsman draws 1 extra navigation card into it before choosing.
- In anyone else's hands it does nothing; give it to the helmsman. Laying it in front has only one benefit: the Child cannot steal it.

@provision life_preserver

- It only works when laid in front of you: going overboard deals you no damage for falling in, and it is not washed away itself; and if your health drops to exactly 0 in the water, you fall unconscious instead of drowning.
- It does not protect against Chum.
- You can give it away as normal, and at the moment people go overboard you can also throw it straight to a conscious person in the water; see Chapter 9.

@provision parasol

- You spend your action to play the Parasol and open it (if it is in your hand, it is laid in front first). If it is only laid in front and never opened with an action, it does nothing.
- Once open, it covers 1 thirst a day, for you only, and it stays open until you go overboard and it is washed away.
- If you give the Parasol to someone else, they have to spend an action to open it again themselves.

# Chapter 13: The Weather Cards {#section-68}

One weather card is turned over at the start of each day, and it lasts the whole day. Used weather cards go to the discard pile; when the weather deck is empty, the discards are shuffled back in.

@weather huge_wave

If the navigation card played today carries the fight icon, everyone who fought today goes overboard instead, as a separate group, and they are no longer thirsty today for having fought.

@weather sweltering

Today each thirst takes 2 Water to cover. The Hostess needs 2 cadged Water to cancel 1 thirst. If someone passes Water to another person but it does not make up a full 2, the Water passed is returned.

@weather becalmed

There is no navigation phase at all today: nobody can row, no navigation card is turned over, and there are no gulls, no overboard and no thirst. If the rowing pile has cards in it, the whole pile goes back to the bottom of the navigation deck, and the day ends as usual.

@weather scorching_heat

Today everyone still alive (unconscious people included) has one extra source of thirst.

@weather clear_skies

No other effect today. But when this card is turned over, the weather discards are shuffled back into the weather deck.

@weather dense_fog

Today the gulls on navigation cards do not count at all, whether they add one or take one away. Gulls turned up by someone firing a Flare Gun do not count either.

@weather storm

If the navigation card played today carries the oar icon, everyone who rowed today goes overboard instead, as a separate group, and they are no longer thirsty today for having rowed.

@weather gale

In today's navigation phase, first turn over one extra card from the top of the navigation deck and resolve all of it, gulls, overboard and thirst, before the helmsman picks a card as usual. Across the two cards, each source of thirst still counts only once a day, and an open Parasol still covers only one thirst a day.

@weather rain

Nobody in the boat is thirsty today. None of the five sources of thirst count.

@weather sunday

In today's provision phase, after the Supply Crate has gone round once, there is a whole second round straight away.

# Chapter 14: Reading a Navigation Card {#section-69}

@navstats

## The Three Sections {#section-70}

Every navigation card is divided into three sections, from top to bottom: gulls, overboard, thirst. They are resolved in that same order.

- **Gulls**: one more gull, one fewer, or left blank.
- **Overboard** and **Thirst**: who is named. This can be:
  - one or more particular characters;
  - everyone in the boat;
  - everyone except certain people;
  - whoever drank Rum today (this only appears in the overboard section);
  - nobody at all.

If a card names a character who is not in this game's roster, or who has already been taken by the sea, that name is simply ignored.

## The Two Icons {#section-71}

- **Oar**: everyone who rowed today gets one thirst from this card.
- **Fight**: everyone who fought today gets one thirst from this card.

If the card played does not carry the matching icon, rowing or fighting today does not make you thirsty. But on Huge Wave and Storm days, these icons send the people concerned overboard instead (see Chapter 13).

## Reading a Card {#section-72}

When you are helmsman and choosing a card, ask yourself three things. Will this card bring the gulls to 4? Whom will it send into the water? Whom will it make thirsty, and do they have Water in hand right now?

# Chapter 15: Ten Things New Players Get Stuck On {#section-73}

1. **Wounded, you still fight at full strength.** Fights always use your full Size, so losing health does not make you weaker (Chapter 8).
2. **A tie goes to the side that was targeted, and everyone on the losing side gets hurt.** Think it through before you take sides to help someone: if they lose, you lose 1 health too (Chapter 8).
3. **A weapon face up in front of you does not count unless you commit it.** In a fight, only the weapons you commit add to your strength; just having them out in front is no use (Chapter 8).
4. **Unconscious people still get thirsty.** They cannot drink on their own, so they rely on someone conscious to pass them Water (Chapter 9).
5. **You can drink Water that is in front of you.** Water laid in front can still be drunk or passed to others, so laying it in front does not waste it (Chapter 9).
6. **Rowing or fighting does not always make you thirsty.** It depends on whether the navigation card finally played carries the matching icon; but drinking Rum always makes you thirsty that day (Chapter 9).
7. **In the water, dropping to exactly 0 health means death.** In the boat, 0 health only means you fall unconscious; in the water, without a Life Preserver to protect you, it means you drown (Chapter 9).
8. **A Life Preserver or an Oar only works in front of you; a Compass works in hand too, but only for the helmsman.** (Chapter 12)
9. **Once the provision deck runs out, it is gone.** Used provisions are never shuffled back, so sooner or later the Supply Crate comes up empty (Chapter 6).
10. **Every character has exactly one person who loves them and exactly one who hates them.** Once you work out who loves someone, you know nobody else in the boat does (Chapter 4).
