# Chapter 1: The Night

The mirror has taken you back to the night of 14 April 1912. In a few hours the Northern Star will go down. You will be squeezed into a small boat, as one of the people aboard it.

## What You Do in a Game

- There are 6 to 8 people in the boat. Each plays one character. You only find out who you are when you wake in the fog.
- Each day, gulls may fly in out of the fog. Once there are 4 gulls, the boat comes ashore and the game ends.
- If everyone in the boat dies, the game also ends.
- At the end, everyone scores. The highest score wins. Ties are possible.

## Surviving Is Not Winning

Your score has four sources, each separate from the others:

1. you are alive;
2. the treasure you carry;
3. the person you love is alive;
4. the person you hate is dead.

At the start, everyone is given one Love card and one Hate card. Only you can see yours. They are turned over at the very end, in the Reveal.

So you must keep the boat going until it reaches land, while quietly protecting one person and working against another. Best not to let anyone see whom you are protecting.

## How Long a Game Lasts

There is no limit on the number of days. When the gulls reach 4 depends on the navigation cards, and on what the helmsman picks. Most games last about ten days.

# Chapter 2: Before You Board

## Through the Mirror

Find the Magic Mirror in your own world and right-click it. The Northern Star keeps your inventory for you, and you arrive in front of the mirror on the A Deck landing.

To go home, right-click the mirror aboard the Northern Star. You get your inventory back and arrive in front of the mirror you came through. If that mirror is gone, you are sent to your respawn point.

The Northern Star is everyone's lobby. You cannot break anything aboard. Feel free to walk around the ship.

## Signing Up

Boat No. 1 hangs on the boat deck. It is the drill boat. Sitting in it signs you up; standing up cancels. The boat has 8 seats.

## Ringing the Bell

Once 6 to 8 people are seated, anyone sitting in the boat can ring the bell beside it (or right-click the boat again). With too few or too many people, you are only told how many are seated.

Whoever rings the bell chooses the roster for this game. Use a 6, 7 or 8-player preset, or pick the characters yourself, as long as their number matches the people seated in the boat and no character appears twice.

@presets

Once the roster is set, the characters are dealt out at random to the people in the boat. Only those seated in the boat are taken into the game.

## When a Game Is Over

After the Reveal, everyone returns to the Northern Star. To play again, sit in the drill boat again.

# Chapter 3: The Eight in the Boat

## Seats

Each character has a fixed seat, numbered from bow to stern. The order is the same whoever plays the character. In smaller games some seat numbers are empty; only the order from bow to stern matters.

## Size and Survival

Each character has two numbers.

**Size** governs four things:

- Your strength in a fight. It does not drop when you are hurt.
- Your maximum health. You start with health equal to your Size, and lose 1 health for each point of damage.
- The points scored by whoever hates you, once you are dead.
- The bigger you are, the more often navigation cards name you for thirst.

**Survival** governs two things:

- The points you score yourself if you are alive at the end.
- The points scored by whoever loves you, if you are alive.

An easy way to remember it: whoever wants you alive scores your Survival; whoever wants you dead scores your Size.

- Cadging rum: when someone else drinks Rum, she gets a share: Size +3 in fights that day, and 1 thirst that day as well. However many people drink, it counts once; if she also drinks her own that day, it is still only +3.

# Chapter 4: The Two in Your Heart

At the start, everyone is given two secret cards: one Love and one Hate, each naming a character. Only you can see yours.

- Every character is loved by exactly one person and hated by exactly one person. So if you work out who loves someone, you know that nobody else does.
- You may draw yourself.
- Your Love and your Hate may be the same person.

These two cards never change during the game, and nobody else sees them until they are turned over one by one in the Reveal. For how they score, see Chapter 11.

# Chapter 5: How a Day Goes

## The Four Phases

Every day runs in this order:

1. **Weather**: turn over a weather card. It lasts the whole day.
2. **Provision**: the Supply Crate is passed from bow to stern, and each person keeps 1 provision.
3. **Action**: starting from the bow, each conscious person does one thing.
4. **Navigation**: the helmsman picks a navigation card to play: gulls → overboard → thirst.

Once navigation is resolved, the day is over, and the next day starts with the weather. Day 1 also starts with the weather.

## In Your Hand and in Front of You

Your provisions are kept in two places.

- **In your hand**: only you can see them.
- **In front of you**: everyone in the boat can see them. Moving a card from your hand to in front of you is called **laying it in front**.

Laying a card in front does not use your action and can be done in any phase, but it cannot be undone. Once a steal reaches the moment of choosing a card, the person being robbed can no longer lay cards in front.

Laying cards in front has an upside: many provisions only work when they are in front of you. It also has a cost:

- when you go overboard, the waves wash away every card in front of you except a Life Preserver;
- when someone steals from you, they can choose a particular card from in front of you.

## What Others Can See

Your health, every card in front of you, and how many cards are in your hand (but not what they are). Nobody can see your Love and Hate, or what the cards in your hand are.

## Giving Cards

During the action phase, any conscious person may give cards to others at any time, as many as they like, without using their action.

- A card given from your hand goes into the other player's hand. A card given from in front of you goes in front of them, still face up.
- The recipient can be anyone still in the boat, unconscious or dead included.
- To trade, each side gives the other a card.
- While a fight is under way, no cards can change hands.

You cannot give cards in the provision or navigation phase. The navigation phase has just two exceptions: throwing a Life Preserver to someone overboard, and passing Water to someone who is thirsty (see Chapter 9).

## Who Is 'Nearest the Bow'

How the crate is passed, whose turn it is to act, and who is helmsman: all of these count only people who are conscious and online. The unconscious, the dead and the disconnected are skipped, but they still occupy their seats.

Seats go by where people sit now. When someone swaps seats, the order behind them changes straight away.

Thirst is the one exception: it resolves in the characters' fixed order, with the Hostess last.

# Chapter 6: Provisions

## The Supply Crate

- The conscious person nearest the bow opens the Supply Crate: they draw cards from the provision deck equal to the number of people conscious right now.
- They keep 1 in their hand and pass the rest to the next person. Everyone keeps 1, all the way to the end.
- Only the person holding the crate can see what is in it. Everyone can see how far it has been passed and how many cards are left.
- The passing order is fixed the moment the crate is opened. If someone falls unconscious along the way, the crate does not change course.

## Time Limit

Whoever holds the crate is on a timer: 2 seconds for each card left in it, but never less than 20 seconds. When time runs out, the card you are looking at is kept for you.

## When the Deck Runs Out, It Is Gone

The provision deck is never reshuffled. If there are not enough cards, draw what is left; the people at the end get none. Once no cards remain, the provision phase is skipped every day from then on.

On a Sunday, after one full round, there is a whole second round.

# Chapter 7: Actions

## Whose Turn

It is the turn of the person nearest the bow who is conscious, online and has not yet acted today. Each person gets one action a day.

The order is live. Someone swapped towards the bow who has not acted yet takes the next turn. Someone who has already acted and is moved further back does not get another turn. Someone revived today who has not acted yet can still take a turn that day.

You have 60 seconds. If time runs out, you do nothing.

## Five Choices

On your turn, choose one:

1. **Row**
2. **Swap seats**
3. **Steal**
4. **Use a provision**
5. **Do nothing**

## Row

- Draw 2 cards from the navigation deck. Draw 1 more for each Oar face up in front of you.
- Look at them, then put 1 face down on the rowing pile. The rest go back to the bottom of the deck.
- Only you know which card you put down. Everyone knows how many cards are in the rowing pile.
- Anyone who rows is marked as having rowed for the rest of the day, which may count for thirst (see Chapter 9).

You have 20 seconds to choose. If time runs out, all the cards you drew go back to the bottom of the deck and none go on the rowing pile, but you still count as having rowed.

You cannot row on a Becalmed day.

## Swap Seats

1. Go back to the world, look at the person you want to swap with, and right-click them.
2. If they are conscious, they are asked whether they agree. If they agree, you swap at once. If not, a fight breaks out, and you swap only if you win.
3. If they are unconscious or a corpse, they count as agreeing, and you swap at once.

You cannot pick yourself, or anyone already taken by the sea.

## Steal

1. Go back to the world, look at the person you want to steal from, and right-click them.
2. If they are conscious, they are asked whether they agree. If not, a fight breaks out; if you lose, you get nothing.
3. If they agree, if you win the fight, or if they are unconscious or a corpse, you choose one card:
   - either name a card in front of them, which goes in front of you, still face up;
   - or draw one at random from their hand, which goes into your hand.

If they have no cards at all, you get nothing. You have 20 seconds to choose. If time runs out, one card is drawn at random from their hand for you; if their hand is empty, you take the first card in front of them.

The Child steals by different rules; see Chapter 3.

## Picking a Target

After choosing Swap seats or Steal, you go back to the world to right-click the person. You have 20 seconds.

- During this time everyone in the boat sees you glowing, and hears that you want to swap seats or steal, but not whom you are after. This is time for the others to bargain.
- When the Child steals, he does not glow and nothing is announced.
- You can cancel and go back to choosing an action. This does not use up your action.
- If you pick nobody within 20 seconds, you do nothing.

Once you have picked someone, this uses your action for the day, whatever the outcome.

## Use a Provision

Only four provisions are used by spending your action: the Medical Kit, the Parasol (to open it), the Shared Ration and the Flare Gun (as a signal). They can be played from your hand or from in front of you. No other provision can be used this way.

For how each one works, see Chapter 12.

## Do Nothing

That is it for today.

## Three Things That Do Not Use Your Action

- **Laying in front**: at any time (see Chapter 5).
- **Giving cards**: at any time during the action phase (see Chapter 5).
- **Drinking Rum**: at any time, once per person per day (see Chapter 12).

# Chapter 8: Fights

## When a Fight Breaks Out

Only in three cases:

- the person you want to swap seats with is conscious and refuses;
- the person you want to steal from is conscious and refuses;
- someone plays the Shared Ration and another player objects.

You cannot start a fight just because you dislike someone.

## First, an Answer

The person targeted has 20 seconds to decide whether to agree. If time runs out, they agree.

## Taking Sides

Until the sides are settled, anyone in the boat who is conscious, online and not yet in the fight may join a side.

- People who have already acted can join, and so can people with nothing to do with the matter.
- Joining does not use your action.
- Once you join, you cannot leave or switch sides. Declaring yourself neutral counts for nothing.
- The unconscious and the dead cannot join.

Taking sides lasts 20 seconds. Each time someone joins, the timer is reset to 8 seconds.

Everyone can see who is on each side and each side's total Size.

## Committing Weapons

Anyone in the fight can commit a weapon.

- Committed weapons are face down: everyone only hears that 1 card was committed, not what it is.
- Only committed weapons count. A weapon face up in front of you does not count unless you commit it.
- When the fight is over, committed weapons are shown: those already in front of you are used first; any more are turned up from your hand and placed in front of you. From then on they stay face up.

Committing weapons lasts 20 seconds. Each time someone commits one, the timer is reset to 6 seconds.

## Who Wins

Each side's strength = the Size of everyone on that side + Rum drunk today + committed weapons.

- Size counts in full; being hurt does not reduce it.
- If the two sides are equal, the defending side (the one targeted) wins.
- On the losing side, everyone in the fight takes 1 damage. The winning side takes none.

After the fight:

- If the attacking side wins: a seat swap happens at once; a steal goes on to choosing a card; a Shared Ration takes effect as normal.
- If the defending side wins: the matter is dropped.

Win or lose, everyone in the fight (including those who took sides) is marked as having fought for the rest of the day.

A committed Flare Gun is discarded after the fight. Other weapons stay in front of their owners.

## What Else You Can Do in a Fight

You can lay cards in front (except the person being robbed, once the steal reaches the moment of choosing a card), and you can drink Rum. You cannot give cards.

# Chapter 9: Navigation

As soon as the action phase ends, the navigation phase begins. Who goes into the water, who is thirsty and whether the gulls come are all settled in this phase.

## The Helmsman

The helmsman is the conscious, online person sitting nearest the stern. The helmsman is not fixed: if someone swaps seats, or the person at the stern falls unconscious, the helmsman changes with them.

## Picking a Navigation Card

- Each person who rowed today has put one navigation card face down on the rowing pile. The helmsman picks up the whole pile, looks through it and picks one to play.
- If the helmsman has a Compass in hand or in front, they draw one more card from the top of the deck into the rowing pile before choosing. It is no use if the rowing pile is empty.
- It does not matter whether the helmsman rowed; they still choose.
- If nobody rowed today and the rowing pile is empty: turn over the top card of the deck and play it.
- If the rowing pile has cards but nobody in the boat is conscious: also turn over the top card.

The helmsman has 20 seconds to choose. If time runs out, the card they are looking at is played.

Everyone sees the card played. The cards not chosen go back to the bottom of the deck, and nobody but the helmsman knows what they were. Navigation cards always go back to the bottom of the deck after use, so the deck never runs out.

## What Is on a Navigation Card

Every card has three sections, always resolved in this order:

1. **Gulls**: one more, one fewer, or none;
2. **Overboard**: who goes into the water;
3. **Thirst**: who is thirsty.

Some cards also carry an oar icon or a fight icon. These icons only affect thirst; see below. For how to read a navigation card, see Chapter 14.

## Gulls

The gull count starts at 0 and never goes below 0. The moment it reaches 4, the boat comes ashore and the game ends. The overboard and thirst sections of that card are not resolved.

## Overboard

- Whoever the card names goes into the water. It may name certain characters, everyone, or whoever drank Rum today. A named character who is not in this game is ignored. Corpses in the boat can be named too.
- Everyone who goes in takes 1 damage, except two kinds of people: a conscious Sailor, and anyone with a Life Preserver in front of them. A Life Preserver clutched in your hand does not count.
- Every card in front of someone who goes in is washed away into the discard pile, except a Life Preserver. Cards in hand are not affected. An open Parasol is washed away too.

At the moment people go into the water, the conscious people in the boat can still do two things. When anyone holds a card they could use, this moment lasts 20 seconds.

- **Throw a Life Preserver**: throw a Life Preserver from your hand or from in front of you to a conscious person overboard, as long as they do not already have one in front of them. You can throw it to yourself. An unconscious person cannot catch it; only a Life Preserver already in front of them protects them.
- **Play Chum**: it draws sharks, and everyone going overboard in this group takes 1 more damage. The Chum is discarded. Chum already face up in front of someone overboard counts too, even if that person is you. Neither the Sailor nor a Life Preserver protects against sharks. Several Chum together still add only 1 damage.

The water is more dangerous than the boat:

- If your health drops to exactly 0 after the damage and you have no Life Preserver in front of you, you drown outright instead of just falling unconscious.
- If your health drops below 0, you drown, Life Preserver or not.

Those who drown are taken by the sea. The cards in their hand and in front of them sink with them, and they are gone for the rest of the game. Those who do not drown climb back into the boat, take their old seats, and are still in time for this card's thirst.

## Thirst

Five things can make you thirsty today. Each counts at most once a day, and they can stack:

- you rowed, and the card played carries the oar icon;
- you fought (helping someone else counts), and the card carries the fight icon;
- the card names you;
- you drank Rum today. This one ignores the card;
- today's weather is Scorching Heat.

For each thirst, drinking 1 Water deals with it; if you do not drink, you take 1 damage. Whether to drink, and how many, is up to you. You can drink Water from your hand or from in front of you.

- When it is your turn to resolve, other conscious people can pass you Water to cover your thirst.
- Unconscious people still get thirsty, but cannot drink on their own; they rely on others to pass them Water. The dead do not get thirsty.
- An open Parasol covers one thirst for you each day.
- The Hostess always resolves last. Each Water drunk by someone before her cancels one of her thirst.

Thirst goes one person at a time, in the characters' fixed order, not by where people sit now. Anyone who is not thirsty at all today does not have to wait. Each person has 20 seconds; if time runs out, you drink the number you have selected.

## What the Weather Changes

In Dense Fog the gulls do not count, in Rain nobody is thirsty, on a Becalmed day there is no navigation phase at all, in a Gale an extra navigation card is resolved… See Chapter 13 for the details.

## The Day Ends

Once the navigation phase is resolved, the day is over. The marks for having rowed, fought or drunk Rum are all cleared. An open Parasol stays open. The next day starts with the weather.

# Chapter 10: Health, Unconsciousness and Death

## Three States

You start with health equal to your Size. Each point of damage takes away 1 health.

- Health above 0: **conscious**.
- Health exactly 0: **unconscious**.
- Health below 0: **dead**.

In the water there is one more rule: if your health drops to exactly 0 and you have no Life Preserver in front of you, you die outright (see Chapter 9).

## Unconscious

An unconscious person:

- cannot act, refuse anyone, or fight;
- cannot give cards, lay cards in front, drink Rum, or drink Water on their own;
- still gets thirsty, and relies on others to pass them Water;
- counts as agreeing when someone swaps seats with them or steals from them;
- can be revived with a Medical Kit.

An unconscious Sailor is still hurt by going overboard. An unconscious person who goes overboard without a Life Preserver to protect them drowns.

## Healing

- **Medical Kit**: one person regains 1 health. You can treat yourself, or revive someone unconscious. Only the wounded and the living can be treated.
- **Shared Ration**: every conscious, wounded person regains 1 health. The unconscious regain nothing and are not revived.

The dead cannot be brought back. Damage is only ever reduced by healing.

## Corpses in the Boat

Anyone who dies in the boat leaves a corpse:

- it still occupies its seat;
- others can swap seats with it or steal from it, and it cannot refuse;
- the treasure on it still counts towards that player's score at the end;
- the Shared Ration can only be played while there is a corpse in the boat.

If a navigation card sends a corpse overboard, it is taken by the sea along with its cards.

## Disconnecting

Disconnecting does not change your wounds or switch off your ability. While disconnected you cannot act: your turn is skipped; if the Supply Crate is in your hands or you are the helmsman, the default is chosen for you when time runs out; when you are thirsty, you can only wait for others to pass you Water.

## Only One Left

If only one person in the boat is still alive, the game still goes on.

# Chapter 11: Landfall and the Reveal

## When the Game Ends

- The gulls reach 4: the boat comes ashore. This can happen in the navigation phase, or in the action phase when someone fires a Flare Gun.
- Everyone in the boat is dead.

Then comes the Reveal.

## The Reveal

1. First, **Hate**: in order of final score from lowest to highest, each person's Hate card is turned over, one at a time.
2. Then **Love**: in the same order, each person's Love card is turned over, one at a time.

Both of everyone's cards are turned over, including those of people taken by the sea. Equal scores go in final seat order.

After that comes the scoreboard: everyone can see each person's total and who scored highest; only you can see your own four-part breakdown.

## Scoring

Score the four parts separately, then add them up:

1. **You survived**: score your Survival. Unconscious counts as alive.
2. **Treasure**: if your body is still in the boat (alive or as a corpse), all the treasure in your hand and in front of you scores. If you were taken by the sea, it is 0.
3. **Your beloved survived**: score their Survival, whether or not you are alive.
4. **Your enemy died**: score their Size. It counts wherever they died, even if they were taken by the sea.

For what each treasure is worth, see Chapter 12.

## Special Cases

- **You love yourself**: parts 1 and 3 are both your own Survival. If you are alive, you score it twice.
- **You love and hate the same person**: if they are alive you score part 3, if they are dead part 4. You can only score one of the two.
- **You hate yourself**: you do not score part 1, even if you are alive. Part 4 becomes the total Size of every other corpse in the boat, not counting yourself or the person you love. Those taken by the sea do not count.
- If you both love and hate yourself, your Survival counts only once.

## Who Wins

The highest total wins. If several people tie for highest, they all win.

# Chapter 13: The Weather Cards

One is turned over at the start of each day and lasts the whole day. Used cards go to the discard pile; when the deck is empty, the discards are shuffled back in.

@weather huge_wave

If the navigation card played carries the fight icon, everyone who fought today goes overboard instead, as a separate group, and is not thirsty for having fought.

@weather sweltering

Each thirst takes 2 Water to cover. The Hostess needs 2 cadged Water to cancel 1 thirst. Water passed to someone that does not make up a full 2 is returned.

@weather becalmed

No navigation phase today: no rowing, no navigation card, no gulls, no overboard, no thirst. The whole rowing pile goes back to the bottom of the deck, and the day ends as usual.

@weather scorching_heat

Everyone still alive (unconscious included) has one extra source of thirst today.

@weather clear_skies

No other effect. When it is turned over, shuffle the weather discards back into the weather deck.

@weather dense_fog

Gulls on navigation cards do not count today, whether adding one or taking one away. Gulls turned up by a Flare Gun do not count either.

@weather storm

If the navigation card played carries the oar icon, everyone who rowed today goes overboard instead, as a separate group, and is not thirsty for having rowed.

@weather gale

In the navigation phase, first turn over one extra card from the top of the deck and resolve all of it, gulls, overboard and thirst, before the helmsman picks as usual. Across the two cards, each source of thirst still counts only once a day, and a Parasol still covers only once a day.

@weather rain

Nobody is thirsty today. None of the five sources count.

@weather sunday

In the provision phase, after one full round, there is a whole second round.

# Chapter 14: Reading a Navigation Card

@navstats

## The Three Sections

From top to bottom: gulls, overboard, thirst. They resolve in that order too.

- **Gulls**: one more, one fewer, or blank.
- **Overboard** and **Thirst**: who is named. This can be:
  - one or more characters;
  - everyone;
  - everyone except certain people;
  - whoever drank Rum today (overboard section only);
  - nobody.

A named character who is not in this game, or who has already been taken by the sea, is ignored.

## The Two Icons

- **Oar**: everyone who rowed today has one thirst on this card.
- **Fight**: everyone who fought today has one thirst on this card.

On a card without the icon, rowing or fighting does not make you thirsty. On Huge Wave and Storm days, these icons send people overboard instead (see Chapter 13).

## Reading a Card

When you are helmsman and choosing, ask yourself three things. Will this card bring the gulls to 4? Whom will it send into the water? Whom will it make thirsty, and do they have Water?

# Chapter 15: Ten Things New Players Get Stuck On

1. **Wounded, you still fight at full strength.** Fights use your full Size (Chapter 8).
2. **A tie goes to the defending side, and everyone on the losing side gets hurt.** Think before you join someone's fight (Chapter 8).
3. **A weapon face up in front of you does not count unless you commit it.** Only committed weapons count (Chapter 8).
4. **Unconscious people still get thirsty.** They cannot drink on their own, so they rely on you to pass them Water (Chapter 9).
5. **You can drink Water that is in front of you.** Laying it in front does not waste it (Chapter 9).
6. **Rowing or fighting does not always make you thirsty.** It depends on whether the card played has the matching icon; drinking Rum always does (Chapter 9).
7. **In the water, dropping to exactly 0 health means death.** In the boat it only means unconsciousness (Chapter 9).
8. **A Life Preserver or an Oar only works in front of you; a Compass works in hand too, but only for the helmsman.** (Chapter 12)
9. **Once the provision deck runs out, it is gone.** Sooner or later the Supply Crate comes up empty (Chapter 6).
10. **Every character has exactly one person who loves them and exactly one who hates them.** Work out who loves someone, and you know nobody else does (Chapter 4).
