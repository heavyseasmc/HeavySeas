# Creative Tabs and the Decorator's Hammer

## Creative tabs

The blocks are grouped by use across eight tabs: structure · mouldings · doors and glazing · stairs and railings · lamps · furniture · deck fittings · ship's features; the ship's boats have a tab of their own. Within a tab, pieces are sorted into matching sets by material.

Search knows the common trade names: "baseboard", "dado" and "crown moulding" all find the right piece.

## The decorator's hammer

The hammer changes a piece after it is placed. Right-click a block with it and that block (the whole piece, for multi-block pieces) switches to its next form; the new form's name shows above the hotbar. It works on:

- the sill of a whole window: inside → outside → both → none
- the back face of a wall block: finished ↔ exterior white
- mahogany capping: plain ↔ tall; mahogany pilaster: left half → centred → right half
- ceiling lights: one cell → two cells east–west → two cells north–south → four cells (a form with no room beside it is skipped)
- the ivy on a trellis, the rug on a deck chair, the sitter in a portrait, which side a davit carries its boat

Sneak and right-click a porthole to lock it as a single porthole, or unlock it so it merges again.

# Materials

- **White exterior wall**: block · stairs · slab. Outside walls, deckhouses, bulwarks.
- **White interior wall** and **white wainscot**: walls indoors. Wainscot is the bottom course.
- **Mahogany**: panelling · wainscot · stairs · slab · pilaster · capping. First-class public rooms and stair halls.
- **Teak decking**: the floor. The planks run the way you are facing when you place it. Its stairs and slabs are for the open decks.
- **Hull plating** in five colours (black · white · red · waterline · buff): block · stairs · slab. The plate seams follow world coordinates, so any size of wall lines up.
- **White ceiling** and **cream ceiling**: ceilings. The ceiling slab makes a drop ceiling or a half-height beam.
- **White tile wall** and **mosaic tile floor**: bathrooms and the swimming bath.

Wall blocks (interior wall, wainscot, framed panels, mahogany panelling) are finished on all four sides, so they look right whichever way they face. Only where the back faces the open air, use the hammer to turn the back to exterior white.

# Walls and Mouldings

A good wall runs, bottom to top: baseboard · wainscot · dado rail · framed panel · plain wall · cornice.

- **Baseboard**, **dado rail** and **cornice** sit in the cell in front of the wall. Neighbours of the same kind join into one run and turn corners by themselves.
- **Pilasters** stand in front of the wall. A dado rail runs across a pilaster; a pilaster that meets a projecting cornice above grows a capital to carry it.
- **Framed panels** (white · gilt line · wide gilt · mahogany · mahogany and gilt) are whole wall blocks, one per cell; neighbours join into one large frame.
- **Door casing** goes beside and above a doorway.

All of these join by looking at their neighbours. Place them and they are right; there is no layering by right-click.

# Doors and Windows

## Whole windows

Whole windows come in nine sizes (1–3 wide by 1–3 high), each one a single item. The sill goes on the side you stand on, and the glass follows the sill; change the sill with the hammer. The windows aboard all have their sills outside.

## Portholes

Portholes of the same kind facing the same way merge into one large round porthole when they make a complete square: 2 × 2, 3 × 3, 4 × 4. Any that do not complete a square stay small. Break one cell and its group comes apart and merges again.

To keep one single, sneak and tap it with the hammer to lock it.

## Doors

Cabin doors are single. Glass double doors are placed side by side; right-click either leaf and both open together.

# Floors and Ceilings

## Carpet

Carpet is a surface on the floor. Right-click a teak deck block with carpet to lay it; right-click with shears to lift it. The carpet works out its own border from its neighbours, so there are no edge or corner pieces to choose. Furniture on a carpeted floor sits right on the pile.

## Ceilings

Lay the ceiling in one sheet. The gilt white ceiling beam hangs half a block below it: where beams meet from both directions the block becomes a cross, and a beam with neighbours on one side turns to follow them. The gilt cornice turns corners like the projecting cornice and joins only its own kind.

# Lamps

- **Ceiling light**: one lamp in the middle of a cell. In a room an even number of blocks wide, use the hammer to make it two or four cells so it sits on the seam, centred.
- **Brass chandelier** and **crystal chandelier**: in the middle of a large room.
- **Wall sconce** on a wall; **floor lamp** and **deck lamp post** on the floor.

Right-click a lamp to switch it on or off.

# Stairs and Railings

- Stairs come in carpeted mahogany, mahogany, teak, white and hull plating.
- **Cast-iron railing** joins its neighbours like a fence and also joins walls and solid blocks. At corners and ends it raises a newel post by itself.
- Beside a stair, use the **cast-iron stair railing** (the sloped one); stand a **brass newel lamp** beside the bottom step.
- Ring a stairwell with cast-iron railing, and fit **well trim** to the face of the floor beside the opening.

# Furniture

- Pieces that span several blocks (sofa, writing table, bed, grand table, two-block lamps) are placed whole from one item, and will not place without room; break any block and the whole piece goes.
- Cabinets open and close with a right-click; pieces with a seat (sofas, chairs, deck chairs, bed edges) let you sit down with a right-click.
- Pieces that stand against a wall (wardrobe, washstand, bookcase) go back to the wall, front facing the room.
- Bathroom fittings (shower, toilet, basin) go back to the wall; the towel rail hangs on the wall.
